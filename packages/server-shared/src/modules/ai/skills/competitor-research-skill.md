# Competitor research: build the vision to outsell, not in a vacuum

When you help this brand set up or sharpen its **vision, positioning, or identity** — its USP, key messaging, target audience, values, tone of voice, or the competitors field itself — do not draft it from the brand's own navel. A vision written without looking at the market is a guess. **Go and look at who they're up against first, assess them, then position this brand to win.** Web search is how you do it (see the web-search skill).

**When this kicks in.** Any time the work touches how the brand presents itself to the market: "help me set up my brand", "sort out my positioning / vision / USP", "who are my competitors?", "how do I stand out / win more customers / outsell them?", "make my messaging better", or when you're proposing a brand-profile fill that includes the positioning fields. If you're about to draft a USP or key messaging and you haven't looked at the competition, that's the signal to search.

## The method

Work through this in order — it mirrors the Infin8 principle of foundation before tactics (you're doing INCUB8/CRE8 work, and it feeds ACCELER8 demand later):

1. **Define the arena.** From the brand's profile, pin down the industry, the target audience, and the geography they actually compete in (a local café competes on a suburb; a SaaS competes globally). If the arena is unclear and you can't infer it, that's a fair thing to ask — but usually you can infer it and proceed.
2. **Find the real competitors — search for them.** Identify a handful of genuine direct competitors (same offer, same audience) and one or two adjacent/aspirational players worth learning from. Use real search; never invent competitor names. Favour ones that actually overlap with this brand's arena, not the biggest names in the industry for the sake of it. Don't forget the **Status Quo** — in many markets, the main competitor is inaction, manual workarounds, or staying with an outdated setup.
3. **Assess each one — pin down their USP, platform exposure & customer friction.** For every competitor, read their public positioning and pull out:
   - **Their USP / core promise:** the one thing they lead with — capture it in a crisp phrase.
   - **Platform exposure & distribution footprint:** where they actually get seen (e.g. Meta ads, Google SEO/PPC, LinkedIn organic, local directories, marketplace reliance like Amazon/Shopify). Note any single-platform dependency or channel vulnerability.
   - **Price posture & offer model:** premium / mid / budget posture, plus offer structure (retainer, quote-based, lock-in contracts vs. pay-as-you-go).
   - **Apparent strengths & customer friction:** apparent strengths plus visible weaknesses, gaps, or recurring complaints mined from public reviews/social proof (slow support, hidden fees, complex setup).
   - **Their proof and tone.** Keep it to what you can actually observe — label anything that's your inference as your read, not fact.
4. **Compare USPs & platform footprints head to head.** Put the competitors' USPs and primary distribution channels side by side with this brand's current positioning. This comparison is the heart of the analysis: it shows where everyone is crowding onto the same promise or relying on the same acquisition platform, and — crucially — which valuable promise or channel **nobody** is claiming.
5. **Find the white space (messaging + channel).** From that comparison, where's the unmet need, the common weakness everyone shares, the promise nobody's making, the audience everyone's ignoring, or the **channel whitespace** (platforms rivals under-utilize or ignore)? That gap is where this brand wins.
6. **Turn it into an outsell strategy.** Translate the white space into a concrete edge: a differentiated **USP** that claims ground competitors left open, **key messaging** that leads with that edge and addresses main market frictions, a **target audience** sharpened to who this brand wins best, **distribution choices** that exploit platform whitespace, and **values / tone of voice** that feel distinct. The test: could a competitor copy-paste this positioning onto their own site? If yes, it's not differentiated enough — sharpen it until it's ownable.
7. **Propose it.** Populate the brand profile via its confirm card (per "When you propose a fill, make it complete"): fill the **competitors** field with the real names you found, and draft the positioning fields shaped by the differentiation. The card is a draft the user reviews and edits — so be bold and complete, not timid.

## Present it well

In chat, before or alongside the card, give the user the intelligence — this is high-value work, so make it land:

- **A USP & platform comparison table** — the centrepiece. Put each competitor next to its USP / core promise, price posture, primary platform exposure, and key weakness/gap, with this brand's USP (current or proposed) in the same table so the contrast is visible at a glance.
- **The white space you found** — one or two sharp sentences naming the messaging and channel openings the comparison reveals: where everyone overlaps, and the promise/platform left unclaimed.
- **The play & counter-positioning** — how the positioning you're proposing exploits that opening to outsell them, including a quick "Us vs. Them" battlecard angle (how to win when a prospect compares this brand to a top rival).
- Then the **confirm card** carrying the drafted competitors + positioning fields. Keep the momentum: this is usually one step in setting the brand up, so tie it into the plan and tee up what's next.

## Honesty guardrails (read these — the earlier web-search caution has a nuance here)

- **Competitor names and public positioning ARE meant to be recorded.** Filling the brand's **competitors** field with real names you found, and drafting a USP informed by what the market looks like, is exactly right — that's what these fields are for. This is the intended exception to "web findings stay out of the profile": the competitors field is *supposed* to hold real, external competitor names.
- **But never fabricate.** Don't invent competitors, don't make up their prices, claims, review counts, or revenue, and don't state an assessment you couldn't actually observe as if it were fact. If you couldn't verify it, say it's your read ("they appear to position as premium").
- **Don't stamp unverifiable numbers into the profile as the brand's own data.** A competitor's rumoured pricing or a market statistic informs your *reasoning and drafts* — it doesn't get written into this brand's profile as established fact. Names and your positioning drafts go on the card; unverified figures stay in the conversation as context.
- **Keep it this brand's voice.** The positioning you draft must sound like this brand (honour its voice fields), even as it's shaped by what the competition is doing.
